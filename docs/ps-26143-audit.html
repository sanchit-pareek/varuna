PS 26143 Conformance Ledger

Every clause of the NTRO problem statement checked against what the TideTrace codebase actually does today, not what it is designed to do. Claims are backed by file, function or measured output, and the two real-world limits that remain are stated as plainly as the clauses that hold. Where an earlier revision of this page overclaimed, the correction is left visible rather than quietly edited out.

Statement 26143 · NTRO Reviewed 6 Sep 2026, rebuilt UI Tests 123 passing, ruff clean Detector in demo U-Net, IoU oil 0.889 Runtime 30-45 s / scene, CPU

Thirteen clauses hold on real data. A fourth scene , Santa Barbara Channel, carries real Sentinel-1, real Copernicus currents, real ERA5 wind, real MarineCadastre AIS and real Sentinel-2 optical 17 hours from the radar pass, with nothing simulated anywhere in it.

Two clauses are partial, and an earlier version of this page called all fifteen met. That was wrong, and the correction matters more than the tally. Look-alike rejection (a.2) works on the dB baseline and returns zero polygons on the shipped U-Net, because the Zenodo ground truth never labelled class 1. EO corroboration (a.4) is corroboration, not the fused second detector the clause could be read to ask for. Both are now marked partial on the clause itself.

Two further limits are facts about available data rather than work left undone. There is no ocean-current model for the Caspian anywhere , verified against Copernicus and HYCOM independently, both of which mask that basin as land. And no paired labelled SAR/optical dataset exists from which to build a fused detector honestly.

a.2

Reject look-alikes Partial

On the baseline path only. The dB baseline separates oil from look-alike on local contrast and boundary regularity , 73 look-alikes on the Gulf scene, 15 on the Caspian, 4 on Santa Barbara. They are returned in a separate array, drawn yellow, and never passed to attribution.

With the shipped U-Net loaded, all four scenes return zero look-alike polygons, and an earlier revision of this page marked the clause met while quoting the baseline's counts as evidence. The cause is stated correctly and the consequence was not: the Zenodo ground truth segments oil only, every look-alike mask measured is all zeros, so class 1 is unsupervised and the trained model cannot predict it. Those chips are used as hard negatives rather than given invented labels, which is the right call and is also why the yellow polygons are only visible when the baseline runs.

a.3

Calculate geometric properties Met

Area, perimeter, maximum Feret length, cross width, PCA orientation, compactness, centroid, bounding box and pixel count , all computed in a local azimuthal-equidistant frame centred on each polygon, so areas are metric rather than degrees-squared.

Detections whose centroid falls ashore are dropped and the count reported. A chip containing coast has dark pixels on it that are not slicks , layover shadow behind a ridge, a calm harbour basin , and nothing upstream of the mask knows the difference. The Caspian scene had two such polygons on the Absheron peninsula: 3 polygons and 0.315 km² becomes 1 and 0.074 km². A polygon straddling the shoreline is kept, which is the right way round to be wrong.

app/geo/geometry.py · land mask from Natural Earth 1:10m land + lakes, 8/8 known points correct · tests/test_land_mask.py

The Caspian limit is real and is not ours to close. That sea is endorheic, so global ocean models carry it as land. Copernicus returns 306 cells and 306 NaNs over the Baku footprint and publishes no Caspian product; HYCOM returns its fill value −30000 at the same point while returning a true −0.48 m/s in the Gulf. Two independent models agree the field does not exist. Rather than let a zero pass as data, the run states that drift there is wind-driven only.

b.2

Trace the slick back to origin point and time Met

Backward Lagrangian ensemble, 50 particles, RK2, hourly steps, drift velocity U_current + 0.03·U_wind10. The origin is the first hour where the 90th-percentile ensemble spread exceeds 8 km, clipped to 6-48 h and buffered 2 km, a rule fixed in advance rather than tuned per scene.

app/drift/, origin zone, ring and hourly envelopes returned per run
b.3

Predict the future flow of the slick Met

Forward ensemble over a 36 h default horizon with hourly envelopes, a forecast cone, a threatened bounding box, and a coastline-impact flag raised by real polygon intersection against the land mask, not a bounding-box approximation.

c.3

Score suspects on proximity, trajectory, behaviour Met

All three named aspects are separate weighted components, plus a vessel-type prior and a temporal term. Scores are an absolute percentage of the weight sum, never min-maxed across the batch, so a scene with no real suspect cannot manufacture a 100% one.

ComponentWeightBasis
proximity0.30exp(−d/R) to the origin point, R the zone radius
behaviour0.25max of four anomaly detectors, gap bonus gated on reception count
time0.20exp(−|Δt|/1.5 h) from the estimated origin time
type0.15published prior for the decoded AIS type
trajectory0.10course alignment at closest approach
× confidence0.35-1.0how much of the track was received rather than dead reckoned

Three of those rows are corrections to a previous revision of this page, which marked the clause met on a model that had two defects worth naming. Proximity decayed on distance to the zone boundary and clamped to zero inside, so on a 227 km² origin zone 8 of 10 Gulf suspects scored exactly 1.00 and the heaviest weight carried no information. There was no temporal term at all, so the ±3 h window was a filter and a vessel three hours early scored like one on the origin minute, which is spatial correlation, not the spatio-temporal correlation the statement asks for. And the AIS-gap bonus was ungated, so the top suspect on the Gulf scene was a fishing boat with two AIS receptions whose track was 99.4% dead reckoning, tied at 73.8% with two others. It now ranks outside the top ten.

app/ais/score.py, raw = 0.30·prox + 0.20·time + 0.25·beh + 0.15·type + 0.10·traj, score = 100·raw/Σw · confidence · pinned by tests/test_scoring.py

Four independent detectors, each contributing a reason string to the verdict rather than an unexplained number: operational-discharge speed band; course change around closest approach; speed drop; and an AIS gap whose bridged segment passes near the origin zone, the going-dark case, which is the one that matters most for attribution.

Expected solution and data , 4 clauses

d.1

Automated ML detection model Met

3-class U-Net++ on a timm-efficientnet-b0 encoder, trained on Kaggle GPU with checkpoint and optimiser-state resume through Hugging Face. IoU oil 0.889, IoU sea 0.982, and 35 of 40 epochs cleared the deployment gate, so the result is stable rather than one lucky epoch.

It took three runs, and the story is worth keeping. The first checkpoint scored IoU 0.857 and painted every scene solid oil; that produced a rebalanced training set and a new gate. The second scored 0.889 and did the same thing. The cause was neither: Zenodo ships its bands as (VH, VV) and the scene fetcher read Planetary Computer assets as (VV, VH). The network trained on (dark, bright) and was asked to predict on (bright, dark). Ordering bands by median brightness , co-pol exceeds cross-pol over water in every sensor and sea state , fixed it with no retraining at all.

app/ml/dataset.py order_bands · gate water_false_oil ≤ 0.02 · both broken checkpoints kept in models/quarantine/ so the claim can be checked
d.2

SAR and EO imagery Partial

“remote sensing satellite data, such as SAR and EO imagery”

Every scene carries a cloud-masked Sentinel-2 L2A chip from the same STAC as the radar, and the pipeline has an EO stage that puts it to work. For each SAR detection it compares the optical inside the polygon against a ring around it: a surface film reads darker than the water round it, a rig or sandbar or ship reads brighter, a low-wind cell reads like nothing at all. Each polygon carries its verdict into the API, the verdict card and the map popup.

It is corroboration and never a detector: nothing is added, removed or reweighted by the check, and the caveat travels with every verdict , Sentinel-2 did not observe this water when the radar did. A genuinely fused SAR+EO model is out of scope on purpose: there is no paired labelled dataset, and inventing supervision for one would repeat the mistake that produced the first quarantined checkpoint.

d.4

A suitable visual interface Met

An operations console rather than a gallery: scene selector with real acquisition metadata, the ten pipeline stages with per-stage timings, a verdict card, a time scrubber over the drift, and cached Satellite / Nautical / Seamark basemap layers so the map reads as a chart offline.

It also shows its own weaknesses. The header badge reads dB BASELINE when no model is loaded, and provenance warnings about simulated AIS or missing currents appear in the panel rather than only in a log.

What is left

  1. The Caspian has no current model in existence, confirmed against Copernicus and HYCOM independently. It is the one input that cannot be obtained by doing more work, and the right response is to keep saying so on every run rather than substituting a plausible-looking field.
  2. Look-alikes are unsupervised in the trained model. The archive’s class-1 masks are measurably empty, so those chips are used as hard negatives rather than given invented labels. The dB baseline still separates look-alikes on contrast and shape, and the UI still draws them.
  3. A fused SAR+EO detector remains a research project rather than an afternoon, for the reason given under d.2.
  4. The live AIS recorder needs to run for a while before its vessels score properly. Static messages carrying vessel type arrive about every six minutes, and type is 20% of the suspicion score.
