The problem

A slick is found. Nobody is charged.

Marine oil spills are detected often and attributed rarely. The radar shows the film hours after the discharge, by which time the slick has drifted and the vessel has left. The link between the two is the hard part, and it is the part this exists to make.

Detect

Segment radar into sea, look-alike and mineral oil. Measure the geometry of what is found.

Hindcast

Run the slick backwards through real currents and wind to an origin zone and an origin time, then forwards to where it is going.

Attribute

Reconstruct the traffic in that zone and window, discard the irrelevant, and score what is left on evidence a human can check.

Architecture

Everything fetched before, nothing fetched during

The part worth telling

Three checkpoints. Two were confidently wrong.

v1, IoU 0.857

Returned ~500 km² of oil on 484 km² chips, the clean-water control included. Training held 400 oil tiles, 150 sea, and zero tiles of plain open water; class weights then pushed 6.25× further toward oil.

v2, IoU 0.889

Rebalanced data, neutral weights, a new gate. Same failure. The cause was neither: Zenodo ships bands as (VH, VV) and the scene fetcher read (VV, VH). Trained on dark-first, asked to predict bright-first.

v3, shipped

Ordering bands by median brightness, co-pol exceeds cross-pol over water in every sensor, fixed it with no retraining at all. The control scene returns nothing.

The lesson is not "we fixed a bug". It is that IoU could not see the failure. Measured only on tiles selected for containing oil, it rises as a model predicts more oil.

water_false_oil, the fraction of genuinely-water pixels called oil, is now a hard gate at 0.02. An epoch above it cannot become the shipped checkpoint however good its IoU, and if no epoch clears it the run writes nothing and says so. On v1 that number was ≈1.0.

The control scene is the real test

Arabian Sea returns no polygons, no drift, no suspects. Every broken checkpoint painted it solid oil. A detector that cannot say "nothing here" is not a detector.

Santa Barbara is the full stack

Real radar, real Copernicus currents, real ERA5 wind, real MarineCadastre AIS, and real optical 17 hours from the radar pass at 0% cloud. Nothing in it is simulated.

Attribution

A ranked suspect, with its reasons attached

ComponentWeightBasis
proximity0.40exp(−d/3 km) to the origin zone boundary
behaviour0.25max of four anomaly detectors
type0.20published prior for the decoded AIS type
trajectory0.15course alignment at closest approach

Scores are an absolute percentage of the weight sum, never min-maxed across the batch, so a scene with no real suspect cannot manufacture a convincing one.

The behaviour detectors

1. Speed inside the operational-discharge band.
2. Course change around closest approach.
3. Speed drop across the window.
4. An AIS gap whose bridged segment passes near the origin zone, going dark, which is the one that matters most.

Every hit emits a reason string: ais_gap_47min_within_2.3km. The funnel is reported too, considered, kept, dropped outside radius, dropped for too few points, so a reviewer can see exactly what was discarded.

The UI says it in the panel and the PDF repeats it: ranked likelihood for investigation, not proof of discharge.

No Caspian current model exists

Copernicus returns 306 cells and 306 NaNs over the Baku footprint and publishes no regional product. HYCOM returns its fill value at the same point while returning a true −0.48 m/s in the Gulf. Two independent models agree the field does not exist, so that scene says wind-driven only on every run rather than showing a plausible zero.

EO corroborates, it does not detect

No paired labelled SAR/optical oil dataset exists to train a fused detector, and inventing supervision is exactly what produced the first quarantined checkpoint. So optical compares inside each polygon against a ring around it and reports agreement. Nothing is added, removed or reweighted by it.

Also stated rather than buried: the archive's look-alike masks are measurably empty, so the trained model's class-1 head is unsupervised and those chips are used as hard negatives. The dB baseline still separates look-alikes, and the UI still draws them.

Why it should be trusted

It reports what it could not determine

Provenance on every run

Which detector ran. Whether the AIS was real or simulated. Whether the basin had currents. How far the optical was from the radar pass.

Failures kept, not deleted

Both broken checkpoints sit in models/quarantine/ with a README explaining what they got wrong, so the claims here can be checked.

The journal

1,748 lines, append-only, 20 entries, including every bug that shipped and was caught, with the measurement that caught it.

The land mask is the clearest example. The coastline file that shipped called the Santa Barbara mountains sea and open Caspian water land, wrong in both directions. Rebuilt from Natural Earth, the first attempt read 144 of 144 sample points across the Caspian as land, because Natural Earth models inland seas as holes and the builder was reading only exterior rings. Now 8/8 known points are correct, and detection drops polygons that fall ashore: the Caspian had two, on the Absheron peninsula.
